The Problem
In many parts of the world, it is still difficult to clearly match farmers to the exact land they own or manage. Records are often split between simple lists and maps that do not fully align. In this challenge, you are given a list of farmers with basic details such as IDs, land size, and group labels A to E. You are also given a map made up of polygons, which are shapes that represent individual plots of land. These two sources are both useful, but they do not perfectly match each other.
This mismatch creates real problems. Farmers need to prove which land belongs to them in order to access programs like carbon credits, which are financial incentives for environmentally friendly farming. When dealing with large areas and many farmers, manually matching each person to a plot of land becomes slow and error-prone. Existing tools are not designed to easily connect simple datasets, like spreadsheets, with spatial data, like maps.
Farmers themselves often have helpful knowledge about their surroundings. For example, they may know which farms are next to theirs or who is located to the north, south, east, or west. This type of information is rarely captured in data systems, but it could help improve accuracy.
The opportunity is to use AI to link farmer records to the correct land polygons, make use of farmer-provided information, and build a solution that works at scale. This could make land mapping more reliable, unlock access to financial benefits, and support better decision-making in agriculture. That is the gap you are here to fill.


Your Challenge
How might we leverage AI to enable accessible smart farming practices for farmers in regions of the world where we can unlock the power of nature the most? 


* This is a model problem. The data presented to you is just a small dataset. In reality this can be multiplied into thousands or tens of thousands of hectares, and hundreds of thousands of farmers.
* There is a list of farmers containing IDs and farm land areas, and we know that they are categorized into five groups, A-E. However, the groupings do not perfectly align with the manually drawn polygons generated from on-the-ground observations by the field staff.
* We assume that both the farmer list and the polygons provided by the field staff are provided faithfully and accurately to the best of their ability. For farmers to be eligible for the benefits of carbon credit registration, aligning data and evidence is important.


The best solutions will be rooted in real data, technically functional, and clearly connected to measurable outcomes. You do not need to solve everything: a focused, well-executed tool is more compelling than a broad but shallow one.

Deliverable Description
A function that can link farmers on a list (text) with fields on a map (polygons) based on key data and responses from farmers about their relative position to one another.
